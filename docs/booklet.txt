1. Project Objective
The purpose of this multidisciplinary project is to develop an optimization-based application and propose an improved or alternative solution approach for a real optimization problem presented in an academic article.
Each student team has been assigned one academic article that presents a specific optimization problem and a solution methodology (algorithm).
Students will:
	•	Carefully study and understand the optimization problem, mathematical formulation, and the solution approach described in the assigned article.
	•	Analyze the algorithm used in the paper and reproduce its main idea if necessary for understanding.
	•	Design and propose an alternative or improved solution method to solve the same optimization problem. The proposed approach may include:
	•	improving the original algorithm,
	•	developing a hybrid algorithm,
	•	applying a different existing optimization/heuristic/metaheuristic algorithm, or
	•	proposing a new solution strategy.
	•	Implement the proposed method in a working software application.
	•	Test their proposed method using the same benchmark datasets used in the paper.
	•	Compare their results with those reported in the article and perform benchmark analysis.
	•	Evaluate whether the proposed method improves solution quality, computational time, or robustness.
	•	Prepare a final report and presentation demonstrating their methodology, implementation, benchmark comparison, and findings.

The main goal of the project is not simply reproducing the paper's algorithm, but developing and testing a new or improved solution approach for the same optimization problem.
This project aims to strengthen students’ multidisciplinary teamwork skills, optimization modeling ability, algorithmic reasoning, and innovation in algorithm design.


2. Team Structure and Roles
👷 Industrial Engineering Students
Responsibilities:
	•	Understand the optimization problem definition (objectives, constraints, assumptions).
	•	Formulate the mathematical model based on the paper.
	•	Analyze the algorithm presented in the paper and develop an alternative or improved solution approach (improved algorithm, hybrid model, or different optimization technique).
	•	Define the structure and logic of the proposed method and communicate it clearly to the CE/SE teammate before implementation.
	•	Take full responsibility for the methodology section of the paper — understanding the proposed algorithm, its logic, and parameter definitions.
	•	Explain the algorithm’s operation to the Computer/Software Engineering teammate and ensure full understanding before implementation.
	•	Prepare flowcharts or pseudocode describing the algorithm’s structure.
	•	Conduct analysis of benchmark results (objective values, convergence, comparisons).
	•	Write the methodological sections of the report.
*** The Industrial Engineering students play a key role in ensuring that the algorithm is correctly implemented. If they cannot explain the problem and methodology clearly, the application will not work properly — and the entire project will fail for both sides. Collaboration and continuous communication between team members are therefore essential.

💻 Software/Computer Engineering Student
Responsibilities:
	•	Translate the algorithm into code based on the Industrial Engineering team’s explanations and pseudocode.
	•	Develop the implementation using appropriate programming tools.
	•	Conduct benchmark testing and record computational results.
	•	Visualize performance metrics (objective trends, runtime, solution quality).
	•	Document and organize the code for reproducibility.
	•	Write the implementation part of the report.
*** The Software Engineering student also carries equal responsibility for the success of the project.If the application is incomplete or does not reflect the algorithm correctly, both sides will be negatively affected. Accurate coding and frequent communication with the Industrial Engineering teammates are therefore critical.

🤝 Joint Tasks (Collaborative Work)
	•	Work collaboratively throughout all stages of the project.
	•	Conduct weekly meetings to synchronize modeling and coding progress.
	•	Validate the algorithm’s correctness using the benchmark dataset.
	•	Prepare the final report and presentation together.
	•	Ensure that both modeling and implementation aspects are integrated coherently.

7. Supervision
To support the progress of the projects, regular academic supervision meetings will be held with the advisor. Due to the high number of teams this year (30 teams), supervision meetings will be organized every two weeks for each team.
Each meeting will last approximately 10–15 minutes and will provide teams with an opportunity to: ask questions about their project, discuss difficulties related to modeling, algorithms, or implementation, receive guidance for the next steps of their work.
Supervision meetings will follow the schedule below:
	•	Teams 1–16 will attend supervision meetings in the first week.

	•	The cycle will then continue in the same order (Teams 1–16 → Teams 17–30 → …).

 8. Academic Integrity

	•	The goal is to understand the original method and develop an improved or alternative solution approach, not simply reproduce the existing algorithm. 
	•	Team accountability will be evaluated during the final presentation.
